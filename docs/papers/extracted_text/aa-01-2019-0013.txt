Optimally scheduling and loading tow
trains of in-plant milk-run delivery
for mixed-model assembly lines
Binghai Zhou and Zhexin Zhu
School of Mechanical Engineering, Tongji University, Shanghai, China
Abstract
Purpose – This paper aims to investigate the scheduling and loading problems of tow trains for mixed-model assembly lines (MMALs). An in-plant
milk-run delivery model has been formulated to minimize total line-side inventory for all stations over the planning horizon by specifying the
departure time, parts quantity of each delivery and the destination station.
Design/methodology/approach – An immune clonal selection algorithm (ICSA) combined with neighborhood search (NS) and simulated annealing
(SA) operators, which is called the NSICSA algorithm, is developed, possessing the global search ability of ICSA, the ability of SA for escaping local
optimum and the deep search ability of NS to get better solutions.
Findings – The modiﬁcations have overcome the deﬁciency of insufﬁcient local search and deepened the search depth of the original metaheuristic.
Meanwhile, good approximate solutions are obtained in small-, medium- and large-scale instances. Furthermore, inventory peaks are in control
according to computational results, proving the effectiveness of the mathematical model.
Research limitations/implications – This study works out only if there is no breakdown of tow trains. The current work contributes to the in-plant
milk-run delivery scheduling for MMALs, and it can be modiﬁed to deal with similar part feeding problems.
Originality/value – The capacity limit of line-side inventory for workstations as well as no stock-outs rules are taken into account, and the
scheduling and loading problems are solved satisfactorily for the part distribution of MMALs.
Keywords Scheduling, Mixed-model assembly lines, Line-side inventory, Loading, Material delivery, Tow trains

1. Introduction
Over the past few decades, the pressure of automobile
industry has been growing as requirements for automobile
products
have
been
gradually
increasing
with
the
development of economy. To satisfy diversiﬁed demands of
individual
customers,
mixed-model
assembly
lines
(MMALs) have been widely adopted in today’s automobile
production (Shahabudeen et al., 2002; Wang et al., 2011).
Under this circumstance, several models and different types
of products are assembled on the same assembly line, and
thus a variety of parts required in assembly line are
indispensable. Because the available space is extremely
scarce (Peng and Zhou, 2018), it is impossible to store all
those parts at the factory in practical applications, let alone
at assembly workstations. Therefore, enormous amount and
diversity of parts have to be transported just in time (JIT) to
multitudinous stations while avoiding shortages, otherwise
any shortage of parts might lead to line stoppage and
hundreds of assembly workers being idle (Zhou and Peng,
2017; Boysen et al., 2015).
In such a context, the in-plant milk-run handling strategy
has been used by an increasing number of automobile
manufacturers for timely and accurate delivery of diversiﬁed
parts to multiple workstations. In-plant milk-run systems
are transportation systems, where materials are delivered
from a central storage area to different destinations on
predetermined routes in short intervals (Klenk et al., 2015).
This naturally puts pressure on the delivery as it requires a
massive amount of frequent small-lot material handling. As
a consequence, to maintain the MMALs well supplied and
ensure
the
smooth
operation
of
production,
JIT
supermarkets
gradually
take
the
place
of
centralized
warehouses. Within this concept, the supermarket is a
decentralized logistics area for stacking materials by the side
of an assembly line, in which materials are replenished by
industrial
trucks
in
large
lots
from
the
centralized
warehouse. When a workstation that the supermarket is in
charge of requires materials, corresponding bins will be in
preparation. These bins contain complete kits of parts for
speciﬁc models. The parts are placed in the same order as
the
workpiece
assembled
along
the
line
to
reduce
unproductive searching and walking time of assembly
workers. (Bozer and McGinnis, 1992; Limére et al., 2012).
Moreover, these comparatively small and standardized bins
can be stored in accessible racks (Finnsgård et al., 2011),
which leads to the ergonomic improvement.
Afterward, bins are transported to workstations by tow
trains, which are wagons with electric towing equipment. First,
the tow train is loaded in the supermarket with bins of materials
required by workstations. Then it sets out to deliver the
corresponding number of bins in sequence to workstations on
the MMAL that are in demand of materials. Finally, at the end
of each tour, the tow train returns to the supermarket to be
replenished for the preparation of the next delivery.
When it comes to tow trains, it should be noted that they
usually operate on a ﬁxed schedule, and the tow train
scheduling is within the scope of in-house logistics. As Emde
et al. (2012) pointed out that the planning and control of this
in-house logistics concept amounted to a complex task
where the following interrelated decision problems had to be
solved:
1
decision on the number and location of decentralized
supermarkets;
2
assignment
of
line
segments
to
supermarkets
and
determine the number of tow trains per supermarket;
3
determination of each tow train’s ﬁxed delivery schedule;
and
4
decision on the bins to be loaded per tour of a tow train.
The ﬁrst two problems are related to the supermarket, such as the
supermarket location determination and deciding the number of
tow trains per supermarket. The last two problems are related to
the tow train, which means the frequency and time of tow trains
leaving the supermarket to tour stations on its given route, the
tow train stopping at which station and the number of bins it
unloading there. As is known to all, a reasonable and
comprehensive schedule plan of material delivery can deﬁnitely
ensure a reliable and steady part supply, but it is hard to be
achieved without taking into account the number of part bins to
be loaded to corresponding stations per tour. Moreover, a well-
scheduled plan can reduce the line-side inventory effectively, and
because space on the assembly shop is scarce and expensive
(Emde and Boysen, 2012), it is necessary to develop a scheduling
plan for tow trains and determine the number of bins to be loaded
on a tow train in each tour. In other words, the paper focuses on
decisions 3 and 4. The tow train scheduling and loading
problems are the most crucial ones in the ﬁeld of part feeding.
Similar to the digital factory that uses the simulation techniques
to realize the design and optimization of production processes
and system (Centobelli et al., 2016; Kuhn, 2006), the scheduling
and loading problems mentioned before use the simulation
method to improve the scheduling process of the material
delivery, so that the corresponding evaluation objective is
optimized, which can make the optimization of the whole
scheduling system come true. It is of great signiﬁcance in the
comprehensive
process
management,
the
model
planning
(Zakoldaev et al., 2019), reducing costs of the manufacturing
system and improving the core competitiveness of the enterprise.
An efﬁcient material handling system can effectively reduce the
operating cost by 20-30 per cent (Zhou and Peng, 2019), second
only to the optimal assembly sequence, which can reduce the
total cost by 40-60 per cent (Bahubalendruni and Biswal, 2018;
Bahubalendruni and Biswal, 2017). Therefore, it is fairly
essential to do some research on the scheduling and loading
problems.

2. Literature review
Although tow trains are widely applied in the automotive
industry, there have been few relevant literatures tackling the
scheduling of tow trains. As mentioned before, Emde et al.
(2012) distinguished between four decision problems related to
organizing the part feeding process with tow trains. To date,
literatures involving the above four decision problems are
introduced.
Regarding
the
ﬁrst
decision
problem,
namely,
the
supermarket location problem, Battini et al. (2012) described
the supermarket concept explicitly and did surveys on basic
decision problems emerging when implanting the supermarket
concept. Emde and Boysen (2012) studied the supermarket
location problem with the consideration of optimizing the
supermarket conﬁguration costs and total material ﬂows, and a
dynamic programming algorithm was obtained. However, the
research did not take the capacity limit of the supermarket and
unavailable
areas
in
the
workshop
into
consideration.
Afterward, Alnahhal and Noche (2015) dealt with the two
aspects, and the time complexity of the SLP problem was
increased. Later, a real genetic algorithm (RGA) was carried
out to handle the problem.
The second, third and fourth decisions, namely, the routing,
scheduling and loading of tow trains, are the subproblems of
the delivery-to-line (DL) problem. According to different
handling vehicles, the DL problem can be divided into two
categories: part distribution by the forklift (DL1) and material
delivery by
tow train
(DL2).
The two
problem
types
correspond to the point-to-point and the milk-run delivery.
About the DL1 problem, Kozan (2000) studied the
assignment
(DL1-assignment)
and
sequencing
(DL1-
sequencing) problem of material delivery tasks for the truck
assembly plant in Australia. The optimization model proposed
by the authors is an extended application of the traditional
multi-period vehicle routing problem (VRP). Boysen and Bock
(2010) focused on Hoist’s material delivery sequencing
problem
(DL1-sequencing),
aiming
at
minimizing
the
maximum inventory in all stations and production cycles with
part-dependent
weights,
and
constructed
a
dynamic
programming algorithm and SA algorithm. Rao et al. (2013)
investigated the scheduling of a single vehicle. To avoid
material shortage and reduce total costs including inventory
holding cost and travelling cost, it was necessary to determine
the departure time and part quantity of each delivery according
to the predetermined assembly sequence. Despite the reference
value of aforementioned literature, it is unrealistic for their
models to be directly applied to the JIT material handling of
MMALs.
The second, third and fourth decisions are relevant to the
part feeding of assembly lines, which are consistent with
material delivery by tow train (DL2). The modeling and
optimization problems of material handling for MMALs in this
paper have certain correlations and similarities with the
traditional VRP (Ho and Haugland, 2004; Baker and Ayechew,
2003; Laporte et al., 1988) and inventory routing problem
(IRP) (Kleywegt et al., 2002; Vidovic et al., 2014), but there are
three differences:
1
Customers in the VRP are visited only once, unlike the
part feeding in assembly lines that requires multiple
delivery.
2
The classic VRP and its extension problems only deal with
path optimization problems without considering the end
demand.
3
The end demand of IRP-related problems is set to a
certain value (or subject to a certain distribution), but the
end demand of the material delivery of assembly lines
problem is a time series.
With regard to the VRP problem, as for the milk-run delivery,
Vaidyanathan et al. (1999) extended the classical VRP model
and proposed a JIT capacitated vehicle routing problem
(JITCVRP) to handle the routing problem of tow trains (DL2-
routing). The authors assumed that once a route was assigned
to a tow train, there was no interruption and the demand rate of
each workstation was constant. To ensure the punctual
delivery, the optimization objective was to minimize total
driving time. The quantity of materials delivered each time
before the next one reaching the workstation was sufﬁcient to
meet the demand of the station. Therefore, shorter delivery
cycle meant less line-side inventory. Similar to the VRP
problem, the DL2 problem was analyzed by Emde and Boysen
(2010). They dealt with the routing of tow trains (DL2-
routing) and the scheduling of material delivery (DL2-
scheduling), and developed a nest dynamic programming
algorithm to minimize the sum of the line-side inventory cost
and travelling cost. On the basis, Emde et al. (2012) focused on
the loading problem of tow trains (DL2-loading), determining
the replenishment of each workstation with the predetermined
maximum capacity of tow trains, demand of workstations and

Because the scheduling and loading problems are proven to
be NP-hard (Fathi et al., 2015), exact approaches only ﬁt small-
scale problems. To settle large-scale problems with NP-
hardness nature within desirable time, many researchers have
resorted
to
metaheuristic
algorithms
which
possess
the
outstanding computational efﬁciency and superior solution
quality. For instance, a novel SA algorithm-based heuristic
(Fathi et al., 2014a), a novel memetic ant colony optimization-
based heuristic algorithm (Fathi et al., 2014b) and a modiﬁed
particle swarm optimization algorithm (Fathi et al., 2015) were
proposed to tackle the scheduling and loading problems of tow
trains, all aiming at minimizing the number of tours and line-
side inventory. In addition, Peng and Zhou (2018) used
artiﬁcial bee colony–based approach to investigate the JIT in-
house logistics problem for automotive assembly lines. The
multi-objective ant colony algorithm (Rubaiee and Yildirim,
2019) was developed to minimize total completion time and
energy cost on a single-machine preemptive scheduling.
Besides, facing the fourth industrial revolution, an improved
binary grey wolf optimizer was proposed by Jiang et al. (2019)
to solve the dependent task-scheduling problem.
Apart from the algorithms mentioned above, there are also a
bunch of algorithms derived from or similar to the classic
genetic algorithm (GA) which generates new candidates by
crossover and mutation operators, such as differential evolution
(DE) algorithm and ICSA etc. For example, Rao et al. (2013)
developed a hybrid genetic algorithm and simulated annealing
(GASA) approach to deal with the scheduling problem with
high efﬁciency even in large instances. Moreover, an elitism
genetic algorithm (EGA) combining an elitism strategy was
proposed by Xie and Chen (2018) address uncertainty job shop
scheduling problem with interval grey processing time. It can
be seen that these algorithms show remarkable performance in
search ability, short computational time and the extensibility,
which provides inspirations that metaheuristic algorithms can
be combined with other factors to obtain stronger effectiveness.
Among these algorithms, the ICSA, which was designed based
on the clonal selection of biological immune system, retains an
outstanding
information
processing
and
problem-solving
ability, which are embodied in the characteristics of distributed,
self-learning, self-adaptation, self-organization and robustness.
It is capable of converging to the global optimal solution rapidly
in the searching process. Owing to its outstanding global search
capability, the ICSA has attracted great attention and been
applied to tackle a number of optimization problems in
scientiﬁc and engineering applications (Zhou and Wu, 2019),
including robotic assembly line balancing and ﬂow shop
scheduling. For instance, regarding the ﬂow shop decision-
making problem, Liu et al. (2009) applied the ICSA to handle
the
hybrid
ﬂow-shop
scheduling
with
the
objective
of
minimizing makespan. Peng and Zhou (2018) solved the JIT
part distribution problem for automobile assembly lines by the
proposed modiﬁed ICSA. Zhou and Wu (2019) developed an
improved immune clonal selection algorithm (MOICSA) to
solve bi-objective robotic assemble line balancing problems,
and the MOICSA showed great search and convergence ability,
which led to better results compared to the other algorithms. As
mentioned before, the purpose of the scheduling and loading
problems tackled in this paper are meant to minimize total line-
side inventory of all stations over the planning horizon, so it can
be seen as a typical optimization problem. In addition, to the
best of the authors’ knowledge, ICSA has not been used to
tackle the tow train scheduling and loading problems in the
literature. Consequently, it is worthier evaluating the ICSA’s
capability of solving the problem tackled in this paper as a
global search algorithm.
However, despite the abovementioned advantages, ICSA
falls short in the local search phase, so in this paper, a novel
intelligent ICSA incorporated with the SA and NS operators,
which the authors call the NSICSA, is developed to solve the
scheduling and loading problems of tow trains with line-side
and tow train inventory capacity constraints. The proposed
NSICSA possesses the global search ability of ICSA, the ability
of getting rid of local optimum of SA and the deep search ability
of NS. Owing to comprehensive advantages, it can enhance the
quality
of
solutions,
effectively
solve
the
problems
of
optimization with constraints and has a fast convergence speed.
Experimental results show that the NSICSA is of better
performance compared with the ICSA in that it has the
advantage of higher quality solutions and outstanding searching
efﬁciency as well as avoiding prematurely converging to local
optimum by means of chaos-based initialization.

4. NSICSA design
As discussed in the above section, the problem in this article
falling under the category of NP-hard brings signiﬁcant
difﬁculty in obtaining optimal solutions for exact algorithms.
Consequently, ICSA is introduced as the main framework for
the optimization algorithm. The ICSA is distinguished for its
excellent data and information processing but comes short on
local search processes. To ameliorate the capability of the
proposed algorithm, NS is incorporated with ICSA to improve
the exploitation ability in local search. And in the meanwhile,
SA is integrated into the hybrid algorithm to avoid local
equilibrium when the algorithm evolves to a certain extend.
Accordingly, in this paper, an ICSA combined with the NS and
SA operators which the authors call the NSICSA is developed,
possessing the global search ability of ICSA, the ability
of getting rid of local optimum of SA and the deep search ability
of NS to get better solutions. The detailed descriptions of
NSICSA are presented as follows.

5.5 Managerial applications
When the number of workstations is considerably large and
the inventory capacity of each workstation needs to maintain
a low level, given the limitation of loading capacity of tow
trains, the speed of driving, loading and unloading and the
time of material replenishment in the supermarket, a fairly
comprehensive
scheduling
method
may not
meet
the
requirement. Consequently, it is necessary to increase the
number of tow trains to carry out delivery tasks. The
workstations need to be partitioned to reduce the driving
time and transmit time of tow trains, so as to achieve an
efﬁcient delivery scheme. The cost of material inventory can
be substantially reduced despite a slightly increase in the
ﬁxed cost. So, in the long run, the total cost can be saved.
Accordingly,
when
implementing
the
comprehensive

demand of the workshop and think over if the number of tow
trains need to be increased, which will reduce the overall

6. Conclusions
In this paper, we have investigated the scheduling and
loading of tow trains of in-plant milk-run delivery for
MMALs. A combined scheduling and loading distribution
model was formulated to minimize total inventory for all
workstations over the planning horizon. We proposed a
novel
optimal
method
NSICSA,
consisting
of
the
mechanism of ICSA with the mechanism of SA and NS. The
solution obtained provided us a suitable timetable for tow
trains and the quantity of bins to be delivered to each
workstation under the precedence constrain. It is observed
that the NSICSA outperforms other methods on the
performance of convergence and the quality of solutions,
especially when the problem scale increases to the real-
world scale. It is able to obtain solutions with lower
variabilities
within
an
acceptable
computational
time.
Furthermore, it veriﬁes the validity of the capacity limit of
line-side inventory. The current work is capable of solving
the scheduling and loading problems of in-plant milk-run
delivery, and it can be modiﬁed to solve similar part feeding
problems.
The problem discussed in this paper is based on the
assumption that tow trains will not break down during the
distribution process, and demands over the planning horizon is
known, but uncertainties are likely to occur in the actual
situation. Therefore, uncertain factors such as tow train
breakdowns, short-term adjustments of production planning
and downtime of the assembly line may be taken into account,
that is to say, the future work will focus on the real-time
scheduling which is dynamically scheduled according to the
system status (such as the demand of each workstation, the tow
train status, etc.), thus having the ability to cope well with the
dynamic environment and having wide applications in the
practical production.

A B S T R A C T  
In automotive mixed-model assembly lines (MMALs), a large number of different parts need to be supplied to the 
assembly lines on time, which poses significant logistical challenges for manufacturers. However, consistently 
supplying parts for MMALs is a very complex issue due to factors such as diverse component requirements and 
logistical coordination in the supply chain. In this paper, we propose a bi-objective optimization problem to 
minimize the line-side inventory and energy consumption in a milk-run material distribution system. Meanwhile, 
the number of Kanban and the capacity of the material bin that affect the scheduling is jointly optimized, so that 
the material distribution scheduling plan is optimized. Considering the character of the problem, a multi- 
objective artificial electric field algorithm with SARSA mechanism (MOAEFASA) is developed to solve the 
problem. The algorithm proposed combines the merits of the artificial electric field algorithm (AEFA) and the 
framework of the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA-II). In addition, several optimization strate­
gies are used to optimize the performance of the algorithm. Finally, the validity of the mathematical model is 
verified through the Epsilon constraint method and the superiority of the MOAEFASA is illustrated by numerical 
experiments with four outstanding meta-heuristics.   
1. Introduction 
Today, automotive assembly systems are faced with formidable 
challenges stemming from the escalating diversity of customer demands. 
In order to maintain sufficient competitivity, it has been more and more 
imperative for manufacturing enterprises to simultaneously enhance the 
flexibility and effectiveness of production lines, promoting the extensive 
application of MMALs (Zhou & Peng, 2019). The material distribution 
problem, as one of the key challenges in MMALs, takes responsibility for 
the timely and efficient delivery of materials to the production line, 
which is paramount for fulfilling Just-In-Time (JIT) production. 
The challenge posed by the JIT material distribution problem lies in 
effectively delivering required parts to specific locations within desig­
nated time windows (Sipahioglu & Altin, 2019). The milk-run system is 
an important means of realizing JIT material distribution. Within this 
system, vehicles commence their journeys from a starting point (such as 
a warehouse or a line-side integrated supermarket), make stops at pre­
determined locations, and ultimately return to the initial starting point. 
Through reasonable scheduling, milk-run systems can obviously reduce 
line-side inventory, hence decreasing inventory costs. Kilic et al. (2012) 
propose three categories for milk-run material distribution problems. 
The first category is the “general assignment problem,” where both the 
route and time periods are unknown. The second is the “dedicated 
assignment problem,” wherein the route is predetermined, but the 
operating time of the vehicle on the specified route remains unknown. 
Lastly, the “determined time periods assignment problem” involves 
known time periods for vehicle travel, but unknown routes. At the same 
time, they gave a mathematical model for the determined time periods 
assignment problem. However, in real factories with MMALs, vehicles 
typically have multiple predetermined routes. The time required for a 
vehicle to deliver parts needs to be determined through scheduling. 
Employing a milk-run system enables companies to establish reliable 
and frequent transportation schedules, facilitating the distribution of 
materials to assembly lines in a JIT manner. This approach ensures a 
steady supply of parts, reduces the necessity for excessive inventory, and 
maintains uninterrupted production. Consequently, exploring solutions 
to the multi-route dedicated assignment problem holds significant 
practical value. 
However, with the growing emphasis on sustainable production 
models and energy-related concerns, solely optimizing inventory costs is

no longer the sole focus of the automotive industry (Briand et al., 2018). 
Instead, the consideration of energy costs in transportation is drawing a 
growing concern. However, MMALs following the JIT principle typically 
distribute materials in small batches, which conflicts with the goal of 
energy conservation. When vehicles are operated with higher frequency, 
energy consumption in transportation increases while inventory costs 
decrease, and vice versa. Therefore, it is of strategic significance and 
practical value to incorporate energy factors when studying the sched­
uling problem of MMALs using the milk-run approach. Based on these 
considerations, the primary motivation of this paper is to concurrently 
optimize the economic and energy-efficient performance of milk-run 
material distribution systems in MMALs. 
In this paper, a milk-run material distribution scheduling problem 
considering energy factors (MMDSPCE) is proposed for MMALs. The 
material distribution schedule for heterogeneous AGVs with defined 
travel routes is developed. Moreover, the Kanban quantity and the ca­
pacity of material bins that affect the scheduling plan are jointly opti­
mized to realize the optimization of the material distribution schedule. 
When formulating the material distribution schedule, the two objectives 
of minimizing total line-side inventory (TLI) and total energy con­
sumption (TEC) are considered together, which correspond to the JIT 
principle and energy efficiency standards respectively. Due to the non- 
deterministic polynomial (NP-hard) nature of the problem, a multi- 
objective artificial electric field algorithm with SARSA selection mech­
anism (MOAEFASA) is developed to generate the optimal dispatch plan 
of the problem by considering the delivery route, delivery time, and 
material loading. MOAEFASA is a hybrid algorithm of AEFA and NSGA- 
II, in which fast non-dominated sort and crowding distance are applied 
to select offspring. Moreover, the SARSA selection mechanism is intro­
duced to select the optimal local search scheme and improve the local 
search ability of the algorithm. Besides, a diagonal linear random 
initialization strategy is proposed in the phase of population initializa­
tion to increase the diversity of the population. Finally, experimental 
results show that the proposed MOAEFASA outperforms the compara­
tive algorithms, and the obtained solutions have better Pareto fronts. 
This paper makes the following four contributions:  
(1) The MMDSPCE for MMALs is proposed. To meet the requirements 
of JIT delivery and energy saving together, the energy con­
sumption target is incorporated into the milk-run material dis­
tribution problem. Consequently, a bi-objective problem is 
formulated, aiming to simultaneously minimize TLI and TEC. 
Additionally, a joint optimization is conducted for the Kanban 
quantity and the material bin capacity, both of which impact the 
efficiency of the milk-run system.  
(2) A mathematical model for the optimization of the proposed 
MMDSPCE is established. Moreover, the accuracy of the model is 
demonstrated with the help of the Epsilon constraint method.  
(3) Considering the complexity of the problem, this paper proposes a 
multi-objective intelligent optimization algorithm, MOAEFASA, 
to solve the MMDSPCE. To improve population diversity and 
local search capability of the algorithm, several optimization 
strategies are proposed.  
(4) The validity and superiority of the algorithm are verified through 
the comparison experiment with four multi-objective optimiza­
tion algorithms. Additionally, a specific instance is employed to 
exemplify managerial applications, illustrating the practicality 
and significance of the proposed model and solution method. 
The remainder of the study is organized as follows: Section 2 presents 
the literature review. Section 3 describes the problem and introduces the 
mathematical model and related property theorems. Section 4 develops 
the MOAEFASA algorithm. Section 5 conducts computational experi­
ments to evaluate and verify the performance of MOAEFASA. Finally, 
the study is summarized in Section 6 and an outlook for future research 
is presented. 
2. Literature review 
In recent years, due to the gradual increase of product customization 
and renewal requirements, the research on the material distribution 
scheduling problem of MMALs has become more and more urgent, and 
the material distribution system, as one of the most vital parts, has 
attracted extensive attention from scholars (Emde & Schneider, 2018; 
Hou et al., 2023). Furthermore, the milk-run distribution approach, as 
demonstrated by Domingo et al. (2007), can successfully standardize the 
material distribution process, making it easy to manage and control in 
practical implementation. Up till now, the application of milk-run dis­
tribution systems in plants has been thoroughly investigated. Kilic et al. 
(2012) systematically partitioned the problem based on distribution 
routing and cycle time. Then, with the goal of minimizing the fixed and 
variable costs of vehicles, they developed the mathematical models of 
the material distribution scheduling problem with fixed distribution 
times on a single route and multiple routes. Moreover, Ciemnoczolowski 
and Bozer (2013) pointed out that in a stable milk-run distribution 
system, the number of Kanbans and the physical volume of vehicles have 
a greater impact on the shortage of workstations, so they studied the 
calculation method of the number of Kanbans in the milk-run distribu­
tion systems. However, their research is limited to the single-route and 
single-vehicle milk-run distribution systems. Further, Faccio et al. 
(2013) studied how to apply Kanban and optimize Kanban quantities in 
multi-model assembly lines. In this study, the product BOM and pro­
duction mix variations are considered. In addition, Hanson and Finns­
gård (2014) studied the effect of material bin capacity on material 
supply efficiency, but they only conducted a qualitative study. Sipa­
hioglu and Altin (2019) established an integer programming model for 
an in-plant milk-run distribution system to determine automatic guided 
vehicle (AGV) distribution routes and cycle time. Their proposed model 
allows for split deliveries at the workstations and considers increasing 
AGV capacity by adding trailers. Besides, an excel-based method for 
optimizing milk-run distribution systems was designed by Francuz and 
B´anyai (2022). They determined a complete distribution route by 
establishing routes between delivery objects. Zhou and Zhao (2022) 
fully considered the impact of fixed usage cost and variable operation 
cost of EVs on production cost. They developed a mathematical model 
for scheduling EVs in the milk-run material supply system, aligning with 
energy-saving objectives by minimizing the quantity of EVs and their 
maximum driving distance. However, their study focused on the EV 
handling process without considering the amount of line-side inventory. 
Miqueo et al. (2023) developed a discrete event simulation model based 
on the milk-run distribution model using an industrial case study of a 
global white goods manufacturing company. They simulated four sce­
narios and presented the simulation results. Finally, Facchini et al. 
(2023) investigated the practical integration of a milk-run system with 
an internet of things system, proposing a dynamic routing strategy for 
the planning of towing trains. An algorithm developed is proposed and 
applied to a real case study of a global automotive company. Summa­
rizing the above literature findings, the core issue that needs to be 
considered in a milk-run distribution system is determining the material 
distribution route and cycle time. However, factors such as Kanban 
quantity, material bin capacity, handling vehicle capacity, material 
distribution method and the introduction of new systems or technologies 
all have great impacts on the scheduling plans formulated. In particular, 
Kanban quantity and material bin capacity will have a direct impact on 
how stable the assembly system is, thus considering their combined 
optimization is crucial to maintaining the stability of the assembly sys­
tem and reducing the line-side inventory. However, there is currently 
very little literature that considers the influence of these two factors 
together in the milk-run distribution system. 
From another perspective, the Kanban quantity and the material bin 
capacity also have impacts on the frequency of material distribution, 
however, higher distribution frequency also means increased energy 
consumption. How to achieve the trade-off between the two is an urgent

challenge that needs to be addressed today. Nowadays, with increas­
ingly stringent government requirements for sustainable development, 
more and more scholars have conducted research on reducing the en­
ergy consumption of discrete manufacturing systems (Diaz & Ocampo- 
Martinez, 2019). For instance, Zhou and Shen (2018) proposed a ma­
terial distribution scheduling problem considering energy consumption. 
They designed an automatic material handling system that uses a group 
of AGVs to complete the material distribution tasks. Finally, under the 
condition of ensuring the stability of the assembly systems, the control of 
the energy consumption of the material distribution process is realized. 
Barak et al. (2021) studied the energy consumption of AGVs in resource- 
constrained flexible manufacturing systems. A linear energy consump­
tion function based on load and distance is established. Zhou and Zhu 
(2021) used mobile robots in the material distribution process. They 
took the total energy consumption of mobile robots as one of the goals, 
and hoped to design an energy-saving scheduling system. Vahedi-Nouri 
et al. (2022) discussed a bi-objective cooperative capacity-constrained 
electric vehicle routing problem, aiming to minimize total cost and 
total electrical energy consumption. B´anyai (2023) investigated the 
application of drones for in-plant transportation of small parts. They 
proposed a joint AGV-Drone in-plant supply model, which makes 
possible the joint service of AGV-based milk-run vehicles and drones for 
in-plant supply. Akkad and B´anyai (2023) described how to transform 
the traditional in-plant supply solution based on milk-run operation to a 
physical networked milk-run operation supply. An approach to in-plant 
supply systems based on industry 4.0 technology is proposed. A math­
ematical model with the goal of minimizing energy consumption and 
carbon emissions is given. The proposed approach involves the intro­
duction of manufacturing execution system and the application of in­
dustry 4.0 technologies such as digital twins, which have good research 
and application prospects. In addition, the relationship between the 
energy consumption of AGVs and factors such as load, distance, time, 
and acceleration have all been considered, and the energy consumption 
models for which were also established respectively (Briand et al., 2018; 
Zhang et al., 2014). To sum up, while paying attention to the stability of 
the assembly systems, research on the energy consumption index of the 
distribution process is equally important. 
In terms of the solution method, since the proposed MMDSPCE is a 
classical NP-hard problem, it will take a lot of time to solve using the 
exact algorithm when the problem scale is large (Boysen & Emde, 2014; 
Emde et al., 2018; Kilic & Durmusoglu, 2013; Kilic et al., 2012). In 
recent decades, meta-heuristic algorithms have been used more often to 
solve complex optimization problems due to their excellent computa­
tional efficiency. Therefore, many well-known meta-heuristic algo­
rithms have been proposed and widely used to solve NP-hard problems, 
such as genetic algorithm (GA) (Tasoglu & Ali Ilgin, 2023), particle 
swarm optimization (PSO) (Wang et al., 2023). In addition, the AEFA is 
a swarm intelligence algorithm that simulates the motion of charged 
particles in an electrostatic field. This algorithm was originally proposed 
by Anita and Yadav (2019), whose main features include few optimi­
zation parameters, fast convergence, and high accuracy (Cao et al., 
2022). Subsequently, Anita et al. (2020) extended the AEFA for con­
strained optimization problems by introducing new velocity and posi­
tion constraints based on the original AEFA. Izci et al. (2020) proposed 
an artificial electric field with Nelder-Mead algorithm, which combines 
AEFA with the simplex method. They compared the proposed algorithm 
with the original AEFA and several improved AEFAs to verify the 
effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. Besides, Bi et al. (2022) pro­
posed an AEFA with inertia and repulsion, which introduced an inertia 
mechanism and mutual repulsion between charges based on the original 
AEFA. They applied the proposed algorithm to solve the spherical 
mining spanning tree problem and the results proved its superiority. 
Moreover, Niroomand (2021) applied the improved AEFA to solve the 
linear assembly line balance problem for a single type of product. They 
use the classical simulated annealing algorithm as a local search 
mechanism, which effectively improves the local search ability. 
Adegboye and Deniz Ulker (2023) proposed a hybrid AEFA using cuckoo 
search algorithm and refraction learning. The cuckoo search method and 
refraction learning are used to improve the global exploration capability 
and local exploitation potential of the underlying AEFA algorithm. 
Furthermore, since the proposed problem is a bi-objective optimi­
zation problem, the basic single-objective optimization algorithms 
cannot meet the computational demands. Since Coello (2006) first 
introduced evolutionary algorithms to solve multi-objective problems, 
many classic multi-objective optimization algorithms have been pro­
posed to solve practical engineering problems (Elmi et al., 2023; Hou 
et al., 2023). In recent years, some new multi-objective optimization 
algorithms and their variants have been shown to have superior per­
formance. Peng and Zhou (2019) proposed a hybrid bi-objective gray 
wolf optimization algorithm that combines a decomposition framework 
and a gray wolf optimizer when solving a bi-objective truck scheduling 
problem. Petwal and Rani (2020) proposed a MOAEFA. They utilized 
Pareto strength for fitness assignment and adopted a fine-grained elite 
selection mechanism to maintain population diversity. Furthermore, 
Naderipour et al. (2021) used the MOAEFA to configure wind turbines in 
power distribution systems. They aimed to reduce power losses and 
voltage deviations simultaneously. The numerical experiments showed 
that it outperforms MOGWO and MOPSO. In addition, Premkumar et al. 
(2022) proposed a multi-objective equilibrium optimizer for complex 
optimization problems, which combines a non-dominated sorting 
strategy and a crowding distance mechanism to ensure population di­
versity. After verification on 33 problems, the results demonstrate that 
the proposed algorithm performs well in terms of solution efficiency, 
robustness and exploration ability. More innovatively, Liu et al. (2022) 
proposed an adaptive multi-objective differential evolution algorithm 
based on the SARSA method. They applied the SARSA mechanism to 
adaptively select appropriate constraint processing techniques and 
generate strategies. Experimental results also showed that it has the best 
overall performance in solving specific types of multi-objective optimi­
zation problems. Zhou and Zhao (2023) proposed an adaptive artificial 
bee colony algorithm enhanced by deep Q-learning network (AABC- 
DQN) in solving the milk-run vehicle scheduling problem based on the 
supply hub model considering parts rationing. To extend the search 
scope, they propose to utilize deep Q-learning network to select the 
domain search operator. 
However, according to the No Free Lunch (NFL) theorem (Wolpert & 
Macready, 1997), no single algorithm is suitable for any scheduling 
problem. Each algorithm has its advantages and limitations. Therefore, 
in this study, an efficient hybrid algorithm is proposed to solve the 
MMDSPCE. First, a diagonal linear random initialization strategy is used 
to generate the initial population. Then, four local search operators are 
proposed, and the SARSA mechanism is introduced for selection, which 
effectively improves the local search ability of the algorithm. Finally, 
two repair mechanisms are designed to fix infeasible solutions.

Based on the problem description above, the Kanban quantity NKi 
and the material bin capacity Cbi of part i can have a significant impact 
on the scheduling plan developed. Therefore, optimizing the Kanban 
quantity and the material bin capacity for each part is considered in this 
paper to achieve the optimization of the material distribution system. In 
general, on the one hand, the higher the Kanban quantity of a part, the 
higher its line-side inventory. While on the other hand, when the 
maximum actual loading capacity of a bin is reduced, the amount of line- 
side inventory is effectively reduced with the same Kanban quantity. 
More specifically, when parts have higher Kanban quantities and lower 
bin capacities, material is delivered more frequently and therefore 
consumes significantly more energy, but the number of line-side in­
ventories stays low. However, when parts have lower Kanban quantities 
and higher bin capacities, the number of line-side inventories is higher, 
but the number of part replenishment conditions triggered is signifi­
cantly lower, so less energy is consumed. In summary, the MMDSPCE 
studied in this paper establishes and optimizes the material distribution 
scheduling scheme by setting the optimal Kanban quantity and material 
bin capacity for each part, achieving the goal of reducing line-side in­
ventory and reducing energy consumption in the distribution process. 
3.2. Problem assumption 
To formulate the problem effectively, the following assumptions are 
proposed. 
(1) The production plan and material requirements of each work­
station are determined in advance.  
(2) The cycle time is taken as the basic time unit of the assembly line. 
(3) The AGVs and the border of the line for workstations have ca­
pacity constraints.  
(4) The loading and unloading times of a material bin are known and 
fixed.  
(5) Alternative material distribution routes are known and fixed.  
(6) After the completion of one material distribution task, AGVs shall 
wait until the beginning of the next production cycle to execute 
the next material distribution task.  
(7) The AGVs can distribute materials only when the minimum load 
requirements are met.  
(8) The AGVs travel at a constant speed.

In the model, the objective functions (5)-(7) contain two optimiza­
tion objectives, which are minimizing the TLI on the assembly lines and 
the TEC of AGVs. Constraint (8) calculates the variation of line-side 
inventory with production cycle time. Constraint (9) calculates the 
weight of the parts distributed for the workstations. Constraint (10) 
indicates that in each production cycle, at most one AGV distributes 
materials in each route. Constraint (11) ensures that in each production 
cycle, each AGV distributes parts for at most one route. Constraint (12) 
determines the time for an AGV to perform a material distribution task. 
Since this paper assumes that AGV distributes the material at the 
beginning of the production cycle, nvt is a minimum integer representing 
the interval between the two material distribution tasks performed by

4. The proposed MOAEFASA algorithm 
The milk-run material distribution problem with single objective has 
been proven to be an NP-hard problem. This paper delves into the bi- 
objective milk-run material distribution problem. Building upon this 
foundation, we also take into account the influence of Kanban quantity 
and material bin capacity. Consequently, the proposed problem has a 
larger solution space, which makes it difficult to find the optimal Pareto 
solution using the exact algorithm in finite time. Therefore, a multi- 
objective artificial electric field algorithm with SARSA selection mech­
anism (MOAEFASA) is proposed to solve the problem. Section 4.1 de­
scribes the overall framework of the algorithm. Sections 4.2–4.7 discuss 
the entire content of the algorithm. 
4.1. Framework of MOAEFASA 
Fig. 2 shows the algorithm framework of MOAEFASA. First, the 
diagonally linear random strategy is used to quickly generate a series of 
well-diversified initial populations. Then, according to the physical 
characteristics of charged particles moving in the electric field, the po­
sition change of each individual in the solution space is realized. Next, 
four local search operators are proposed, and the SARSA selection 
mechanism is introduced to select the best local optimization scheme to 
improve the local search ability of the algorithm. Finally, according to 
the problem constraints and coding principles, two repair mechanisms 
are proposed to repair the infeasible solutions. In addition, the selection 
strategy of NSGA-II is introduced. By calculating the Pareto level of all 
solutions and the crowding distance at the same level, the pre-NP in­
dividuals are retained as the new offspring. When the number of itera­
tions reaches the maximum, the algorithm ends and outputs the Pareto

For meta-heuristic algorithms, the selection of the initial population

obtain a large number of initial populations in a short time but also 
obtain high-quality initial populations, so as to improve the performance 
of the algorithm. In order to quickly obtain the initial population with 
good diversity and uniformity, this paper introduced the diagonal linear 
initialization strategy to generate the initial population according to the 
problem attribute (Li et al., 2021). The pseudocode for the diagonally 
linear random initialization strategy is shown in Fig. 5. This strategy is 
not only easy to operate but can also greatly improve the diversity and 
uniformity of the initial population. More importantly, its performance 
does not decline as the number of dimensions increases, so it can 
perform well in multi-objective problems. 
4.4. Search strategy based on AEFA 
AEFA is a population-based meta-heuristic algorithm proposed by 
Anita and Yadav (2019). Inspired by Coulomb’s law, AEFA simulates the 
motion of charged particles in an electrostatic field. Therefore, the 
search strategy based on AEFA is described as follows. In the search 
space, each charged particle represents a feasible solution, whose 
strength is measured by the amount of charge it carries. The larger the 
amount of charge, the closer the particle is to the theoretical optimal 
value. Each charged particle is acted upon by the attraction of other 
particles, which, according to the laws of motion, can form a net force 
and an acceleration in that direction. The velocity and position of the 
charge can be calculated from the acceleration of the particle. The po­
sitions and fitness functions of charged particles determine their charge 
and unit mass. Finally, when the amount of a certain charge in the search 
space is large, other charges will be attracted by the large charge so as to 
get closer to the direction of the large charge. Thus, the algorithm 
converges to the optimal solution. Therefore, the process of searching 
for the optimal solution can be visualized as the process of the charged 
particle with a large charge attracting all the other particles with smaller 
charges to it.

4.5. Local optimization strategy based on the SARSA selection mechanism 
In the basic AEFA, only K in equation (27) is used to conduct local 
search, which leads to the local optimization of the algorithm easily. 
Therefore, to improve the local search ability of the algorithm, four 
optimization operators are designed to optimize the algorithm. Local 
optimization repeats NLS times, and the SARSA mechanism is used to 
select the best local optimization scheme, so as to effectively improve 
the local search ability of the algorithm. Among them, the SARSA is a 
reinforcement learning algorithm proposed by Rummery in 1994 
(Rummery & Niranjan, 1994). The SARSA uses the actual Q value to 
update, which is carried out in the order of (st, at, rt+1, st+1, at+1). The 
specific update mode is represented by equation (36).

under the current state, rt+1 represents the reward obtained after 
selecting action at, st+1 and at+1 respectively represent the subsequent 
action, Q(st, at) represents the expected reward value of the action at 
selected by state st, α ∈[0, 1] is the learning rate, γ is the attenuation 
factor, which represents the decay of future reward. The pseudocode of 
the SARSA selection mechanism is shown in Fig. 6.

4.6. Selection strategy based on Pareto domination 
To keep the population size unchanged after each iteration, it is 
necessary to select the pre-NP individuals as the new parent population 
from the mixed population of parent and offspring. Then, the Pareto 
level and crowding distance of NSGA-II are used to select the offspring 
population. The general principle of selection is as follows. Individuals 
with low Pareto levels are preferentially selected. If multiple individuals 
have the same Pareto level, individuals with large crowding distances 
are preferred. 
4.6.1. Pareto level sorting 
Firstly, the set di and number ni of individuals dominated by each 
individual i in the population are calculated. Individuals with ni = 0 are 
placed into the set Level1 and remove their dominant relationships over 
other individuals. Then, update the n values of all remaining particles 
according to the newly obtained di. Similarly, the individuals with ni = 0 
now should be put into the set Level2, repeat the above steps until all the 
individuals in the population have been graded.

5.4. Small-scale comparison experiments

the objectives into a constraint, so that the problem becomes a single- 
objective problem with Epsilon constraints. Then by gradually 
reducing the value of Epsilon, the relationship between the two objec­
tives is established. Finally, the optimal Pareto frontier is obtained.
